# FACTS MATTER. YOURS.

## An internal audit addressed to The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints

This page does not speak as God. It speaks from the addressed grain of the Church's published scriptures, published teaching on repentance, and observable institutional acts.

### The bounded condition

This audit concerns one occurrence: a church institution establishes or maintains a house of worship after directly affected people have refused the specific imposition, and the institution has not resolved the harm through genuine consent, departure, or repair.

Opposition alone does not establish that condition. Lawful approval does not disprove it. The complete site record must establish every edge.

### The root retained by that grain

3 Nephi 14:12 states the reciprocity rule and identifies it with "the law and the prophets." Doctrine and Covenants 58:42–43 identifies confession and forsaking as the observable test of repentance. The Church's published Gospel Topics entry adds restitution: repair the hurt or damage caused by the act.

### The derived finding

If the Church knowingly imposes and continues a specific church occurrence on directly affected people while refusing them the reciprocal treatment its own command requires, and it neither forsakes nor repairs that act, then the continuing church occurrence is the continuing breach. Under the Church's own religious classification, it is a sin carried on in the name of the grain the institution claims to obey.

This conclusion is conditional. It is not a finding about every temple, every disagreement, or every person involved.

### Fairview, Texas

The official Church record says the original proposal was reduced through mediation, the Town Council approved the revised plan 5–2 in April 2025, construction began in February 2026, and disagreement continued afterward. Town records preserve public opposition and the municipality's position.

Those facts establish conflict, negotiation, compromise, approval, and continuation. They do not by themselves establish the decisive condition: an unresolved non-reciprocal imposition after the full effects, affected parties, authority, alternatives, and repair have been compared.
